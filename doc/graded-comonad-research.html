1. Plain comonad — baseline for comparison

A plain comonad on a category C is a triple (D, ε, δ) where D: C → C is an endofunctor, and:

  • Counit ε : D ⇒ idC — extracts a value
  • Comultiplication δ : D ⇒ D ∘ D — duplicates context

These satisfy three laws (counit left, counit right, associativity):

(ε ∗ D) ∘ δ = idD [left counit] (D ∗ ε) ∘ δ = idD [right counit] (δ ∗ D) ∘ δ = (D ∗ δ) ∘ δ [coassociativity]

A graded comonad generalises this by replacing the single functor D with a family of functors indexed by a monoid, and making the grades appear explicitly in the types of ε and δ.

2. M-graded comonad — full definition

Let M = (M, ⊗, I) be a strict monoidal category (in the most common case: a monoid viewed as a one-object category; or a preordered monoid; or a semiring's multiplicative monoid).

Definition 2.5 (Fujii 2019, arXiv:1904.08083)

"An M-graded comonad on C is an oplax monoidal functor S : (M, ⊗, I) → ([C, C], ∘, idC)."

Concretely, this functor consists of four pieces of data:

Data

  • Sm An endofunctor Sm : C → C for each object m ∈ M
  • Su A natural transformation Su : Sm ⇒ Sm' for each morphism u : m → m' in M
  • ε A natural transformation ε(S) : SI ⇒ idC (the graded counit, indexed at the monoidal identity I)
  • δm,n A natural transformation δ(S)m,n : Sm⊗n ⇒ Sm ∘ Sn for each pair m, n ∈ M (the graded comultiplication)

The key quote establishing the relationship to plain comonads:

"It should be clear that (M, ⊗, I)-graded comonads on C are nothing but (Mop, ⊗, I)-graded monads on Cop." — Fujii, arXiv:1904.08083

And from a shorter statement in the graded Hoare logic paper:

"Graded comonads are dually defined (i.e., as a graded monad on Cop)." — arXiv:2007.11235 (Graded Hoare Logic and its Categorical Semantics)

3. Counit and comultiplication — indexed forms

Counit

Typed at the monoidal unit I. For each object A ∈ C:

εA : SI(A) → A

Contrast with the plain comonad: εA : D(A) → A. The only difference is that the functor is now specifically SI — the functor at the monoidal identity — rather than D unqualified. When M is the trivial monoid (one object *, * ⊗ * = *, I = *), this collapses back to the ordinary counit.

Comultiplication

Typed at the monoidal product of two grades m, n ∈ M. For each object A ∈ C:

δm,n,A : Sm⊗n(A) → Sm(Sn(A))

The grade m⊗n on the left splits into components m and n on the right — the outer functor carries grade m, the inner carries grade n. This is the oplax direction: a lax monoidal functor would go the other way (Tm(Tn A) → Tm⊗n A), which is the multiplication of a graded monad.

Structure Functor direction Counit / Unit Co/Multiplication
Graded monad lax monoidal M → [C,C] η : A → TI(A) μm,n : Tm(Tn A) → Tm⊗n(A)
Graded comonad oplax monoidal M → [C,C] ε : SI(A) → A δm,n : Sm⊗n(A) → Sm(Sn A)
Plain comonad M = 1 (trivial) ε : D(A) → A δ : D(A) → D(D(A))

Unit laws

With whiskering notation (∗ = horizontal composition with identity):

(ε ∗ Sm) ∘ δI,m = idSm [GC4, left counit] (Sm ∗ ε) ∘ δm,I = idSm [GC5, right counit]

Associativity law

(δl,m ∗ Sn) ∘ δl⊗m, n = (Sl ∗ δm,n) ∘ δl, m⊗n [GC6]

This mirrors the coassociativity of a plain comonad, but now each instance of δ carries explicit grade labels.

4. All six axioms GC1–GC6

The full list, from Fujii Definition 2.5 (arXiv:1904.08083):

  • GC1 idSm = Sidm for each object m in M (functoriality: identity morphism in M maps to identity nat. transf.)
  • GC2 Su' ∘ Su = Su'∘u for composable u, u' in M (functoriality: composition in M maps to vertical composition)
  • GC3 Naturality of δ with respect to morphisms in M: the square δm',n' ∘ Su⊗v = (Su ∘ Sv) ∘ δm,n commutes for u: m→m', v: n→n'
  • GC4 Left counit: (ε ∗ Sm) ∘ δI,m = idSm
  • GC5 Right counit: (Sm ∗ ε) ∘ δm,I = idSm
  • GC6 Associativity: (δl,m ∗ Sn) ∘ δl⊗m,n = (Sl ∗ δm,n) ∘ δl,m⊗n

GC1–GC2 assert that S is a functor from M to [C,C]. GC3 is the oplax naturality square. GC4–GC5 are the counit laws. GC6 is coassociativity.

5. Indexed comonad vs. graded comonad

Fujii defines a second, distinct structure (Definition 2.12):

Definition 2.12 (Fujii 2019)

"A B-indexed comonad on C is a functor S : B → Comnd(C)" where Comnd(C) is the category of comonads on C.

This means each object b ∈ B maps to a full comonad (Sb, εb, δb) on C, and each morphism u : b → b' maps to a comonad morphism. Crucially:

Indexed: εb : Sb ⇒ idC and δb : Sb ⇒ Sb ∘ Sb (each grade b has its OWN counit and comultiplication) Graded: ε : SI ⇒ idC and δm,n : Sm⊗n ⇒ Sm ∘ Sn (grades MIX across comultiplication; counit lives at identity)

The structural contrast:

  • In a graded comonad, comultiplication splits a combined grade m⊗n into two separate components. The monoidal structure of M is essential — it specifies what "combining" means.
  • In an indexed comonad, each Sb is already a full self-contained comonad; the category B only specifies how to transport between comonads along morphisms. There is no grade-mixing in comultiplication: δb : Sb → Sb ∘ Sb stays at the same index b.
"B-indexed comonads on C are equivalent to Bop-indexed monads on Cop." — Fujii, arXiv:1904.08083

The seven axioms IC1–IC7 for indexed comonads parallel GC1–GC6 except they lack the monoidal-mixing condition: the counit and comultiplication are indexed pointwise, with no analogue of GC4–GC6 that mixes grades.

6. Semiring grading in coeffect systems

In Orchard–Petricek and Gaboardi et al., the index set M is taken to be a semiring (R, +, ·, 0, 1), not merely a monoid. The multiplicative monoid (R, ·, 1) drives the comonad structure; the additive monoid (R, +, 0) provides additional structure for context splitting in product types.

Counit and comultiplication with a semiring

εA : D1(A) → A (counit at semiring multiplicative identity 1) δr,s,A : Dr·s(A) → Dr(Ds(A)) (comultiplication indexed by r·s, the semiring product) weakA : D0(A) → I (weakening at semiring zero — zero grade = no resource)

The additive monoid gives a further natural transformation for context merging (conjunction of resource demands), used when two computations share the same variable:

merger,s,A : Dr(A) ⊗ Ds(A) → Dr+s(A)

When this extra structure exists, the graded comonad is sometimes called an exponential graded comonad (by analogy with the !-modality in linear logic, which also has weakening and contraction). The search results describe it: "Graded comonads usually have a semiring structure on their indices, adding extra graded monoidal structure called exponential graded comonads."

Orchard's structural coeffects (indexed comonad variant)

In Petricek–Orchard (ICFP 2014), the model is an indexed comonad on a category of contexts, where the grade r is a vector of semiring elements (one per variable in scope). Their informal account of the type-level content:

"Counit here requires a single copy of the value from the context. Comultiplication splits R times S copies of a value into R copies of a context where each context contains just S copies of the value." — Search summary of Orchard/Petricek coeffect papers (coeffects-icfp14.pdf)

Connection to linear logic bang

A graded linear exponential comonad (GLEC) is a graded comonad on a symmetric monoidal category that additionally gives a monoid homomorphism from the semiring's multiplicative monoid into the composition monoid of symmetric monoidal endofunctors. This is the categorical model of the graded !-modality in graded linear logic.

7. Primary sources

A 2-Categorical Study of Graded and Indexed Monads
Soichiro Fujii · arXiv:1904.08083 · Master's thesis, University of Tokyo, 2016 (posted 2019)
Primary source for Definition 2.5 (M-graded comonad), Definition 2.12 (B-indexed comonad), and all six GC axioms. HTML version readable; PDF is compressed. All direct quotes above are from this source.
Towards a Formal Theory of Graded Monads
Soichiro Fujii, Shin-ya Katsumata, Paul-André Mellies · FoSSaCS 2016 · LNCS 9634
Seminal paper establishing graded monads as lax monoidal functors. Graded comonad is the dual (oplax). Also available at Mellies' page (PDF; WebFetch cannot decompress).
Graded Hoare Logic and its Categorical Semantics
arXiv:2007.11235
Source of the concise quote: "Graded comonads are dually defined (i.e., as a graded monad on C^op)." Also discusses costrong M^op-graded comonads with notation (D, ε, δ, cs).
graded monad — nLab
nLab · community-maintained
Defines graded monad as lax monoidal functor (M, ⊗, I) → ([C,C], ∘, id). States: "The grading idea may also be applied to comonads" — dual definition. Confirms the indexed-vs-graded distinction and lists key references.
Parametric Effect Monads and Semantics of Effect Systems
Shin-ya Katsumata · POPL 2014
Originator of parametric/graded monads. "The dual notion of parametric comonad appeared in work on higher-order model-checking." Source of grades-from-preordered-monoids framing.
Combining Effects and Coeffects via Grading
Marco Gaboardi, Shin-ya Katsumata, Dominic Orchard, Flavien Breuvart, Tarmo Uustalu · ICFP 2016
Formalises effect-graded monads and coeffect-graded comonads with graded distributive laws. Introduces "exponential graded comonad" with semiring addition for context merging. PDF is compressed — not directly quotable from WebFetch.
Coeffects: A Calculus of Context-Dependent Computation
Tomas Petricek, Dominic Orchard, Alan Mycroft · ICFP 2014
Concrete coeffect type system over indexed comonads, with semiring grades for variable usage. Models: flat (εr: Dr A → A at r=1) and structural (context-vector) variants. PDF compressed.
