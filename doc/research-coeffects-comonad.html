Indexed vs Graded Comonads: Orchard's Thesis and the ICFP 2014 Paper

Orchard's Thesis — "Programming Contextual Computations" (2014)

Orchard's Cambridge PhD thesis[1] develops two parallel contributions. First, it surveys and extends how existing work structures contextual computations using comonads. Second — and crucially — it identifies a shape preservation property of standard comonads that restricts their applicability: the outer and inner container shapes must match. To escape this, Orchard introduces indexed comonads, a family of functors indexed simultaneously on input context shape and output context shape, whereas graded comonads only vary over a single index (the grade or coefficient).

Key distinction

A graded comonad is indexed by one element r ∈ ℛ, parametrising how a resource is used. An indexed comonad (Orchard's generalisation) tracks both input and output "shapes," removing shape-preservation and enabling a wider range of contextual analyses including flat (whole-context) and structural (per-variable) coeffects.

The ICFP 2014 Paper — Petricek, Orchard, Mycroft

The companion paper[2] by Petricek, Orchard, and Mycroft ("Coeffects: a calculus of context-dependent computation") builds the full typing calculus. Context is modelled per-variable — each variable x carries a coeffect annotation r expressing how that variable is used. The paper's §5 gives the categorical semantics in three subsections: §5.1 "Interpreting contexts and judgments," §5.2 "Sequential composition," and §5.3 "Splitting and merging contexts." Indexed comonads are the semantic foundation; Section 5 of the paper gives an exact characterisation for both the structural (per-variable) and flat (whole-context) cases.

From the ICFP 2014 summary

"Indexed comonads essentially model single-variable contexts, where counit requires a single copy of the value from the context, and comultiplication splits R·S copies of a value into R copies of a context where each context contains just S copies of the value."

Formal Definition of the Graded (Linear Exponential) Comonad

The definitions below synthesise Orchard et al. ICFP 2019[3], Gaboardi et al. ICFP 2016[4], and the CSL 2025 paper[5]. The 2019 Granule paper and the Mixed Linear/Graded Logic paper (arXiv:2401.17199) give the most complete accessible formulations; the thesis and ICFP 2014 paper contain the full proofs but are accessible only as compressed PDFs.

Index algebra

Definition — Preordered semiring

The grade index set is a preordered semiring ℛ = (R, 1, ·, 0, +, ≤) satisfying:

(R, ·, 1) is a monoid — governs usage and composition.

(R, +, 0) is a commutative monoid — governs splitting and erasure.

· distributes over +, and 0 is absorbing.

Both · and + are monotone with respect to ≤.

From the CSL 2025 paper (Definition 1):[5] "the additive part of the semiring is involved in weakening and contraction, and the multiplicative part in usage and composition."

The graded comonad

Definition — Graded comonad

A graded comonad on a category ℂ indexed by ℛ is a family of endofunctors {□r : ℂ → ℂ}r ∈ R together with two families of natural transformations:

Counit

εA : □1 A → A

Comultiplication

δr,s,A : □r·s A → □r(□s A)

natural in A, for all r, s ∈ R.

Coherence laws

Left counit ε□rA ∘ δ1,r,A = id□rA
Right counit □r(εA) ∘ δr,1,A = id□rA
Coassociativity □r(δs,t,A) ∘ δr,s·t,A = δr,s,□tA ∘ δr·s,t,A

Graded linear exponential comonad (GLEC)

For models of linear logic, □r must also carry symmetric lax monoidal structure on ℂ = (ℳ, ⊗, I). This yields two extra families of natural transformations, called the exponential action (Definition 16 of arXiv:2401.17199):

Weakening wA : □0 A → I
Contraction cr,s,A : □r+s A → □r A ⊗ □s A
Mon. unit mI,r : I → □r I
Mon. tensor m⊗,r,A,B : □r A ⊗ □r B → □r(A ⊗ B)

Contraction must be commutative: cr,s,A = (swap) ∘ cs,r,A.

3

Counit and Comultiplication: Dereliction and the Grade Split

Terminology note. The phrases "forget the annotation" and "split the annotation" appear in informal presentations and user paraphrases of the theory, not as literal Orchard quotations located in the sources surveyed here. The formal terms in the literature are dereliction (counit) and promotion/grade decomposition (comultiplication). The intuition is accurate; the phrasing is informal.

Counit — dereliction at grade 1

εA : □1 A → A

The multiplicative identity 1 is the "do-nothing" grade. A resource annotated with grade 1 may be used straightforwardly — no duplication, no restriction. The counit eliminates the modality at grade 1, extracting the plain value. In the CSL 2025 paper this is described as the dereliction operation: "εA : □1 A → A allows returning from the graded necessity modality to base propositions, semantically forgetting the default grade annotation."[5]

In linear logic terms this corresponds exactly to the dereliction rule !A ⊢ A: one may use a banged formula as a plain formula, "forgetting" the ! modality. Grading refines this: the counit works only at grade 1 (the identity), so other grades do not silently disappear.

Comultiplication — the grade split

δr,s,A : □r·s A → □r(□s A)

The comultiplication takes a resource with compound grade r·s and decomposes it into r outer applications each wrapping a grade-s use. Concretely, in a counting semiring (R = ℕ, · = ×): if you have n·m of A, you split into n copies each containing m of A. From the ICFP 2014 summary: "comultiplication splits R times S copies of a value into R copies of a context where each context contains just S copies of the value."

This corresponds to the linear logic rule that lets you promote inside a bang: if Δ ⊢ A then Δ! ⊢ !A. In the graded setting the analogous sequent rule for coeffect annotations would read: a context annotated r·s can be restructured as r-many uses of an s-annotated context.

Counit ε — "dereliction"

□1 A → A

grade 1 = identity

Eliminates the modality

Use once = use plainly

Comultiplication δ — "grade split"

□r·s A → □r(□s A)

grade r·s = nested use

Refines the modality

r outer × s inner

The weakening and contraction distinction

Note carefully that weakening and contraction are not given by the counit and comultiplication — they use the additive semiring structure instead:

Weakening wA : □0 A → I    — grade 0 = unused
Contraction cr,s,A : □r+s A → □rA ⊗ □sA   — additive split

Weakening uses the additive zero 0 (a resource at grade 0 can be discarded). Contraction uses additive sum r+s (a resource at combined grade r+s splits into two independent uses at grades r and s). The multiplicative pair (1, ·) drives the counit and comultiplication; the additive pair (0, +) drives weakening and contraction.

Semiring Structure and the Colax Monoidal Functor Characterisation

Graded monad vs graded comonad

The nLab[6] and the 2-categorical study by Fujii[7] establish the following functor characterisations:

Graded monad (effects)

T : ℛ → [ℂ, ℂ]

LAX monoidal functor

μr,s : Tr ∘ Ts → Tr·s

η : Id → T1

Graded comonad (coeffects)

□ : ℛ → [ℂ, ℂ]

COLAX monoidal functor

δr,s : □r·s → □r ∘ □s

ε : □1 → Id

The monad's multiplication μ goes into Tr·s (lax direction); the comonad's comultiplication δ goes out of □r·s (colax/oplax direction). "Colax monoidal functor" and "oplax monoidal functor" are synonymous in the literature. Archard et al. and the CSL 2025 paper consistently call it a colax monoidal functor; the 2209.06334 paper uses oplax monoidal functor[8].

Precise characterisation

A graded comonad □: ℛ → [ℳ, ℳ] is a colax monoidal functor from (ℛ, ·, 1) viewed as a one-object monoidal category to ([ℳ, ℳ], ∘, Id) the monoidal category of endofunctors under composition.

Colax coherence maps are exactly ε and δ, and the coherence laws are counitality and coassociativity.

Why a semiring rather than a plain monoid?

A bare monoid (R, ·, 1) suffices for a graded comonad, but coeffect type systems also need structural rules (weakening and contraction). Incorporating them requires the additive structure (0, +). The full semiring (R, 1, ·, 0, +) gives:

Semiring part Structural rule Comonad operation
(R, ·, 1) None — pure usage Counit ε at 1, comultiplication δr,s
(R, +, 0) Weakening (grade 0) wA : □0 A → I
(R, +, 0) Contraction (grade r+s) cr,s,A : □r+s A → □r A ⊗ □s A
Distributivity Context scaling r·(s+t) = r·s + r·t coherences

When the semiring is a natural number semiring ℕ, grade r means "used exactly r times," grade 0 is dead code (weakened away), and grade r+s means "used in two independent branches with grades r and s." When the semiring is the Boolean semiring {0,1}, the system recovers ordinary linear typing. When the semiring has a top element ω (or ∞), grade ω means "unrestricted" and recovers the intuitionistic (non-linear) fragment.

Gaboardi et al. 2016 — combining effects and coeffects

Gaboardi, Katsumata, Orchard, Breuvart, and Uustalu[4] show that a language with both effects (modelled by a graded monad Te) and coeffects (modelled by a graded comonad □r) can be combined coherently. The paper introduces graded (monoidal) comonads for coeffects and establishes that the two structures compose when there is a suitable distributive law. The paper confirms the colax monoidal structure for comonads and the semiring requirement on the grade index.

Connections to Graded Modalities in Linear Logic

The standard ! and the linear exponential comonad

Benton, Bierman, Hyland, and de Paiva showed that the ! modality of intuitionistic linear logic is modelled by a linear exponential comonad (LEC): a comonad (!, ε, δ) on a symmetric monoidal category (ℳ, ⊗, I) carrying a commutative comonoid structure on !A for every A, giving weakening (eA : !A → I) and contraction (dA : !A → !A ⊗ !A). Benton's LNL logic[9] decomposes ! as an adjunction L ⊣ R : ℳ ↔ ℂ between a linear category ℳ and a Cartesian category ℂ, so that ! = L ∘ R.

Graded ! as graded linear exponential comonad

The graded !r of graded linear logic is modelled by a graded linear exponential comonad: a graded comonad □ with the additional symmetric lax monoidal structure (weakening at grade 0, contraction at grade r+s) described in §2. From the CSL 2025 paper:

Direct quote — arXiv:2401.17199

"the of-course modality from linear logic can be modelled by a linear exponential comonad and graded of-course can be modelled by a graded linear exponential comonad."[5]

Benton's decomposition — graded version

The CSL 2025 paper generalises Benton's LNL decomposition. Just as ! = L ∘ R for a symmetric monoidal adjunction, the graded □r decomposes as:

□r A = L ( r ⊙ (R A) )

where ⊙ : R × ℂ → ℂ is a strict monoidal action of the semiring ℛ on the linear category ℂ, and L ⊣ R : ℳ ↔ ℂ is the adjunction connecting the graded intuitionistic world ℳ to the graded linear world ℂ. Concretely from the paper (Lemma 3 / Lemma 15): "□r A = Grdr(Lin A)," where Lin maps graded types to linear types and Grdr applies the r-scaling action.

This decomposition is due to Fujii, Katsumata, and Melliès[7] ("every graded comonad can be decomposed into an adjunction and a strict action") and is applied by the CSL 2025 paper to give a mixed linear/graded sequent calculus.

Orchard's Granule language

The Granule project[3] (Orchard, Liepelt, Eades, ICFP 2019) implements graded modal types as a fully-featured functional language. The type system combines coeffect tracking (via graded comonad □r) and effect tracking (via graded monad Te) using a semiring-indexed family of modalities. The Granule language makes the semiring choice programmable: one can instantiate with ℕ (counting), the Boolean semiring (linearity), security lattices (information flow), or interval semirings (approximate computation).

Recent unification — linear-base vs graded-base

The 2026 preprint "Same Coeffect, Different Base"[10] identifies two dominant syntactic approaches: graded-base (graded function arrows A →r B, as in Granule) and linear-base (linear function arrow plus graded modal type □rA, as in graded linear logic). Both share the same underlying semiring and model the same graded comonad; they differ in surface syntax and where the grade annotation sits.
